Privacy Policy

Finding Vending (Find My Drink Japan) · Last updated: 22 August 2026

Finding Vending is a crowd-sourced map of drink vending machines in Japan. You browse machines that other people have photographed, and you can add machines yourself by taking a photo and sharing it. This policy explains exactly what data the app handles, where it goes, and what you can do about it.

The short version: Nothing leaves your phone until you tap Share. When you do, the photo you just took, its location, and your optional notes and rating are uploaded to a server run by the developer and — if the photo passes an automatic “is this a vending machine?” check — the machine’s location is published on the map for every user. Your name and email are never published. There are no ads, no third-party analytics, and no tracking SDKs.

Signing in

The app asks you to sign in so that each submission can be tied to an account (this is what lets us rate-limit abuse and honour deletion requests). You can sign in with Google, Facebook, an email address and password, or continue anonymously (an anonymous account is a random identifier with no name or email attached). Authentication is handled by Firebase Authentication (Google). When you sign in with Google or Facebook, that provider shares your basic profile (name, email address, profile picture) with Firebase; the app uses it only to show that you are signed in. Signing in with a third-party provider is also subject to that provider’s own policy: Google Privacy Policy, Meta Privacy Policy, Firebase privacy and security.

What is collected when you share a machine

Only when you tap Share after reviewing a photo, the app uploads:

DataWhy
The photo you took of the machineShown in the machine’s details and used to check that the photo really shows a vending machine and to identify the drinks it sells.
Location (latitude/longitude) of your device when the photo was takenPlaces the machine on the map. The same coordinates are also written into the photo’s EXIF metadata.
Your notes, rating and the payment methods you selected (optional)Shown in the machine’s details.
The time of the submissionOrdering and housekeeping.
Your account identifier (Firebase user ID)Links the submission to your account for rate limiting, moderation and deletion requests. It is stored with the submission but never shown to other users.

The upload goes over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection to a server operated by the developer, via Cloudflare, which sees your IP address as part of routing the request. The server records the time, your account identifier and the outcome of each upload in its logs for abuse prevention and debugging.

Automatic vending-machine check and publication

Every uploaded photo is scored by an image-recognition model running on the developer’s own server (no third-party AI service is involved). If the model is confident the photo shows a vending machine, the machine’s location, notes, rating, payment methods and photo are published and become visible to every user of the app. If the model is unsure, a human (the developer) looks at the photo and decides. If the photo does not show a vending machine it is not published. Unpublished photos are kept so that the decision can be reviewed and so that a re-sent upload is not processed twice; you can ask for them to be deleted at any time (see below).

The developer may also use submitted photos to improve the recognition models used by the app (for example, to detect which drinks a machine sells). Photos are not sold, not used for advertising, and not shared with third parties for their own purposes.

Where the data is stored

  • Photos and submission metadata are stored on a server operated by the developer.
  • The published machine index (locations, notes, ratings, payment methods, account identifier) is stored in Google Cloud Firestore so that the app can load the map. Users can only read it; the app itself never writes to it.
  • Account data (sign-in method, email address where applicable, anonymous identifier) is stored by Firebase Authentication.

Location

The app asks for location permission to centre the map on you and to record the position of a machine when you photograph it. Your location is used on-device while the app is open and is sent to the server only as part of a submission you share. The app does not track your location in the background and does not keep a history of where you have been.

Camera and photos

The camera permission is used only to take a photo of a machine when you choose to add one. Photos are kept in the app’s private storage on your device until they are uploaded or discarded; the app does not read your existing photo library.

Google Maps

The map is provided by the Google Maps SDK, which loads map tiles from Google and is subject to the Google Privacy Policy.

What is stored on your device

  • Photos awaiting upload, and the upload queue itself (so a submission is retried if you lose connectivity).
  • A local diagnostic log (at most 512 KB, overwritten as it fills) used for troubleshooting on the device. It is never uploaded automatically.
  • Your sign-in session.

Clearing the app’s data or uninstalling the app removes all of the above.

Your control over your data

  • Submissions you have shared are public by design (the machine’s location and photo), but nothing identifying you is shown with them.
  • To have one or more of your submissions removed from the map and deleted from the server, or to delete your account and everything tied to it, use the account deletion request page or email the address below. Requests are processed within 30 days.
  • You can sign out at any time from within the app.

Children

Finding Vending is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new “last updated” date.
